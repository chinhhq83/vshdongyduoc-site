# Omega-3 / fish oil evidence in dogs

## Answer-first summary

**Evidence status: VERIFIED_SUPPORTIVE**

Multiple controlled canine osteoarthritis studies and a systematic review support omega-3-enriched diets or supplements for clinical analgesic or mobility-related outcomes, although dose and product formulation matter.

## What the evidence does not establish

Evidence is strongest for studied omega-3 diets/formulations and does not prove that every fish-oil-containing joint supplement produces the same effect.

Ingredient/formula evidence does **not** prove that every commercial product containing the ingredient is effective. Dose, formulation, co-ingredients, diagnosis, outcome measure and study design can materially affect applicability.

## Key scientific sources

- Evaluation of the effects of dietary supplementation with fish oil omega-3 fatty acids on weight bearing in dogs with osteoarthritis (2010). PMID 20043801. DOI: 10.2460/javma.236.1.67. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20043801/
- Multicenter veterinary practice assessment of the effects of omega-3 fatty acids on osteoarthritis in dogs (2010). PMID 20043800. DOI: 10.2460/javma.236.1.59. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20043800/
- A 2022 Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Enriched Therapeutic Diets and Nutraceuticals in Canine and Feline Osteoarthritis (2022). PMID 36142319. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36142319/
