Boswellia / turmeric / curcumin evidence in dogs

Answer-first summary

Evidence status: INSUFFICIENT

Some canine trials of turmeric extracts or Boswellia-containing combinations report favorable outcomes, but evidence is heterogeneous and often does not isolate the individual ingredient.

What the evidence does not establish

Specific extracts, combination formulas and small samples limit generalization to generic turmeric, curcumin or Boswellia ingredients in commercial supplements.

Ingredient/formula evidence does not prove that every commercial product containing the ingredient is effective. Dose, formulation, co-ingredients, diagnosis, outcome measure and study design can materially affect applicability.

Key scientific sources

  • Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel group study of P54FP for the treatment of dogs with osteoarthritis (2003). PMID 12723628. DOI: 10.1136/vr.152.15.457. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12723628/
  • Clinical efficacy of Curcuvet and Boswellic acid combined with conventional nutraceutical product: An aid to canine osteoarthritis (2021). PMID 34048452. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34048452/
  • Effects of a feed supplement, containing undenatured type II collagen (UC II) and Boswellia Serrata, in the management of mild/moderate mobility disorders in dogs (2024). PMID 39475935. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0305697. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39475935/
